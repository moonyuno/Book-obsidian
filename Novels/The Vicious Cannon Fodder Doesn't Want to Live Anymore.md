> [!synopsis] Synopsis
> Rong Yu dies by suicide and wakes inside an ancient danmei novel as a character who shares both his name and his disability: a sickly young master unable to use his legs, infamous for his vicious temper and twisted personality.
>
> Unfortunately, he has not transmigrated into anyone important.
>
> His half-brother Rong Yue is the novel's beloved protagonist shou: beautiful, kind-hearted, surrounded by friends and destined to attract princes, generals and powerful men wherever he goes. Rong Yu exists primarily as his miserable contrast—a vicious cannon fodder villain whose future ends in brutal revenge at the hands of one of Rong Yue's eventual love interests.
>
> Rong Yu knows exactly what is waiting for him.
>
> He simply does not care.
>
> When he arrives in the novel, he finds the original Rong Yu in the middle of tormenting a servant in the snow. That servant is actually Wei Jingtan, a man currently hiding his identity within the Rong household—and the same man destined to become emperor.
>
> The sensible thing would be to apologise, release him and desperately repair the relationship before Wei Jingtan gains enough power to destroy him.
>
> Rong Yu does the opposite.
>
> With no particular desire to survive and even less interest in playing the role of a repentant cannon fodder, he continues provoking the very man he knows will one day become terrifyingly powerful.
>
> What Rong Yu does not anticipate is that Wei Jingtan is hardly sane himself.
>
> Instead of following the original story, their increasingly dangerous fixation on one another begins pulling the plot off course, turning a predetermined relationship between victim and villain into something obsessive, violent, codependent and unexpectedly difficult for either of them to escape.
