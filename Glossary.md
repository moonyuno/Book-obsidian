## 📖 General Danmei & Novel Terms

**Danmei (耽美 · dān měi)** — Literally "admiring beauty"; the Chinese BL (boys' love) genre of fiction, typically focusing on romantic or erotic relationships between male characters. Originated from Japanese yaoi culture and evolved into its own rich literary tradition.

**Gong (攻 · gōng)** — The "top" or *seme* in a BL pairing; the pursuer or dominant partner in the relationship dynamic. Often depicted as protective, assertive, or deeply devoted.

**Shou (受 · shòu)** — The "bottom" or *uke* in a BL pairing; the pursued or more yielding partner. Can range from gentle and soft to fierce and unyielding — the archetype varies widely across novels.

**CP (cp · couple pairing)** — Refers to the main romantic pairing of a story, or any fan-designated ship. Derived from "couple pairing." e.g., *MXTX's CP in MDZS is WangXian.*

**HE (happy ending)** — The story concludes with the main couple together and flourishing. Highly sought after in emotionally grueling danmei.

**BE (bad ending)** — The story ends in tragedy, separation, or death for the main pair. Notorious examples have broken countless hearts.

**OE (open ending)** — The ending is ambiguous or left to interpretation — neither fully happy nor fully tragic.

**MC (main character)** — The protagonist of the story; usually the POV character.

**ML (male lead / love interest)** — The primary love interest; in danmei, typically the *gong* to the MC's *shou*, though roles vary.

**Manhua (漫画 · mànhuà)** — Chinese comics; the medium in which many danmei novels receive illustrated adaptations.

**Donghua (动画 · dònghuà)** — Chinese animation; the animated adaptation format for popular danmei properties (e.g., *The Grandmaster of Demonic Cultivation*).

**Light Novel / Web Novel** — Danmei is primarily a web novel genre, serialized on platforms like Jinjiang Literature City (晋江文学城). Light novels are shorter, print-formatted editions.

## ⚔️ Genre & Setting Terms

**Wuxia (武侠 · wǔxiá)** — "Martial heroes." A genre set in ancient China featuring skilled fighters, chivalric codes, and the *jianghu*. Lower fantasy than xianxia — powers come from martial training, not spiritual cultivation. Think *Jin Yong* novels.

**Xianxia (仙侠 · xiānxiá)** — "Immortal heroes." High fantasy rooted in Daoism, featuring cultivators who train to achieve immortality, divine realms, demons, and immortal politics. The dominant setting for many beloved danmei works (*MDZS*, *TGCF*, *SVSSS*).

**Xuanhuan (玄幻 · xuánhuàn)** — "Mysterious fantasy." Blends Chinese folklore with foreign fantasy elements (Western magic systems, transmigration, etc.). More free-form worldbuilding than xianxia.

**Cultivation (修炼 · xiūliàn)** — The central practice in xianxia: training one's body, mind, and Qi to accumulate spiritual power and eventually transcend mortality. Stages vary by novel but typically progress from Qi Condensation → Foundation Establishment → Golden Core → Nascent Soul → and beyond.

**Qi (气 · qì)** — Vital energy present in all living things and the natural world. The fundamental resource cultivators refine and weaponize. *Demonic Qi* (魔气) is a corrupted, dangerous variant.

**Jianghu (江湖 · jiānghú)** — Literally "rivers and lakes." The shadow society of martial artists, wanderers, and outlaws operating outside official governance. Governed by its own honor codes and politics.

**Dao (道 · dào)** — "The Way." The central philosophical concept of Daoism — the natural order underlying all existence. In cultivation, one's *Dao* is also their personal path or spiritual truth, which can shatter under extreme emotional trauma (*Dao deviation*).

**Sect (门派 · ménpài)** — A martial arts school or spiritual organization with its own hierarchy, cultivation methods, and territory. Major sects are the backbone of xianxia society (e.g., the Lan Clan of Gusu, the Jiang Clan of Yunmeng).

**Golden Core (金丹 · jīndān)** — A critical cultivation milestone — a condensed sphere of spiritual energy formed in the *dantian*. Its destruction is a catastrophic, often irreversible loss.

**Nascent Soul (元婴 · yuányīng)** — An advanced cultivation stage beyond the Golden Core where the cultivator's spiritual essence coalesces into a separate "infant" entity. Surviving this stage is a mark of great power.

**Dantian (丹田 · dāntián)** — The body's core energy center, located below the navel. Qi is stored and refined here. Destroying another's dantian is considered one of the cruelest acts in cultivation society.

**Spiritual Roots (灵根 · línggēn)** — One's innate cultivation talent — the "roots" that determine how well one can absorb and refine Qi. Characters with no spiritual roots (*sanlinggen*, *wulinggen*) face enormous prejudice.

**Spirit Stones (灵石 · língshí)** — Crystals saturated with spiritual energy; the primary currency among cultivators. Come in low, mid, and high grades.

**Tribulation (天劫 · tiānjié)** — A "heavenly tribulation" — a divine test, usually manifesting as lightning, that cultivators must survive when breaking through to major realm boundaries. Failing means death or regression.

**Dual Cultivation (双修 · shuāngxiū)** — A cultivation method practiced between two people that accelerates spiritual growth. In danmei, this carries heavy romantic and sexual connotations, and is a beloved plot device.

**Immortal Ascension (成仙 · chéng xiān)** — The ultimate goal of cultivation: transcending the mortal realm and achieving true immortality. Often involves passing the final heavenly tribulation.

## 👑 Honorifics & Titles — Sect & Martial Family

These address forms reflect the strict hierarchical bonds of the *shimen* (师门, "master's gate" / sect family).

**Shizun (师尊 · shīzūn)** — "Venerable Master." The most formal and reverent address for one's direct cultivation master. Carries deep weight — disciples use this title for life. (*cf. Shen Qingqiu → Yue Qingyuan in SVSSS*)

**Shifu (师父 · shīfù)** — "Master / Teacher." Slightly less formal than *Shizun* but still deeply respectful. Literally "master-father," reflecting the quasi-parental bond between master and disciple.

**Shixiong (师兄 · shīxiōng)** — "Senior martial brother." A fellow disciple of the same master who entered training earlier. Addressed with deep respect; often a protective or mentoring figure.

**Shidi (师弟 · shīdì)** — "Junior martial brother." A fellow disciple of the same master who entered training later. The *shixiong–shidi* dynamic is fertile ground for danmei tension.

**Shijie (师姐 · shījiě)** — "Senior martial sister." The female equivalent of *shixiong*; an older female fellow-disciple.

**Shimei (师妹 · shīmèi)** — "Junior martial sister." The female equivalent of *shidi*.

**Shibo (师伯 · shībó)** — "Martial uncle (senior)." One's master's senior martial brother — holds higher seniority than one's own master within the generational line.

**Shishu (师叔 · shīshū)** — "Martial uncle (junior)." One's master's junior martial brother — holds lower seniority than one's master but is still called "uncle" by disciples.

**Shizhi (师侄 · shīzhí)** — "Martial nephew / niece." How one addresses a peer's disciple, or how a disciple is called by their master's martial siblings.

**Zhanglao (长老 · zhǎnglǎo)** — "Elder." A senior leadership position within a sect, typically held by experienced cultivators below the sect leader.

**Zhangmen (掌门 · zhǎngmén)** — "Sect Leader." The head of a sect. Literally "keeper of the gate." Elected or inherited; a position of enormous responsibility.

**Zongzhu (宗主 · zōngzhǔ)** — "Clan Leader." The head of a great clan or cultivation family (e.g., *Jiang Zongzhu* for Jiang Fengmian in *MDZS*). Broader than sect leader.

**Daozhang (道长 · dàozhǎng)** — "Daoist Elder / Reverend." A respectful honorific used when addressing Daoist priests or cultivators, especially strangers. A safe, neutral form of address in the *jianghu*.

**Sanren (散人 · sǎnrén)** — "Wandering cultivator." A cultivator without sect affiliation — free but unprotected. Sometimes chosen, sometimes the result of expulsion or tragedy.

## 👨‍👩‍👦 Honorifics & Titles — Family & Social

**Gege (哥哥 · gēgē)** — "Elder brother." Used literally for older male siblings and affectionately by lovers or close friends. One of the most loaded terms in danmei for its intimate overtones. (*"A-Xian, call me gege."*)

**Didi (弟弟 · dìdì)** — "Younger brother." Used for younger male siblings; in danmei, can carry a protective or endearing dynamic.

**Jiejie (姐姐 · jiějiě)** — "Elder sister." Affectionate address for an older female sibling or respected older woman.

**Meimei (妹妹 · mèimei)** — "Younger sister." Used for younger female siblings; can be affectionate or condescending depending on context.

**Gongzi (公子 · gōngzǐ)** — "Young Master / Young Lord." Used to address the son of a noble or official family. Implies refinement, education, and status. A common address for protagonists of noble birth.

**Guniang (姑娘 · gūniáng)** — "Young Lady / Miss." A respectful address for a young unmarried woman. Also used loosely for any woman in period settings.

**Xiaojie (小姐 · xiǎojiě)** — "Miss." An older, formal address for a young lady of status. Note: in modern Mandarin this term has acquired different connotations — context is everything.

**Dage (大哥 · dàgē)** — "Big Brother / Boss." Can be a familial term or a respectful address for an older male acquaintance or gang leader. Warmer and more casual than *gongzi*.

**Furen (夫人 · fūrén)** — "Madam." Respectful address for the wife of a nobleman or official. Carries dignity and social rank.

**Wangye (王爷 · wángyé)** — "Prince / King." Address for a prince of the imperial family. Imperial court danmei revolves around this title.

**Daren (大人 · dàrén)** — "Your Excellency / Lord." Address for imperial officials and authority figures. Indicates rank and formality.

**Xiansheng (先生 · xiānshēng)** — "Sir / Teacher / Mr." A versatile respectful address for an educated or accomplished man. Warmer than *daren*.

**Qianbei (前辈 · qiánbèi)** — "Senior." Used to address someone of greater age, experience, or skill — a sign of respect across sect lines and social situations.

**Wanbei (晚辈 · wǎnbèi)** — "Junior." The reciprocal of *qianbei*; how one humbly refers to themselves when addressing a senior.

## 🏷️ Name Affixes & Suffixes

**A- (阿 · ā)** — An affectionate prefix placed before a name or nickname to indicate closeness and intimacy. Most commonly used between lovers, close friends, or family. (*A-Xian, A-Ying, A-Yuan*)

**-er (儿 · ér)** — A softening diminutive suffix, often applied to children's names or by elders/lovers. Adds a tender, intimate quality. (*Wei-er, Lan-er*)

**-jun (君 · jūn)** — "Nobleman / Gentleman." A respectful suffix for men, indicating refinement or rank. Used in formal address or poetry. (*Wei-jun, Xie-jun*)

**-zun (尊 · zūn)** — "Revered One." A highly deferential suffix marking exceptional status or power. More elevated than *-jun*. (*Hualian's Hua Cheng addressing Xie Lian as* Xianle-zun*)

**-xiong (兄 · xiōng)** — "Brother." Used as a suffix between male peers of roughly equal standing to express camaraderie and respect. (*Wei-xiong, Nie-xiong*)
